AI That Solves a Real Problem

A model is only useful if it answers a question someone actually has. We build practical AI and machine-learning prototypes around your data, your problem and an honest measure of whether the result works.

What We Build

Applied Machine Learning, Not Hype

Prototype-scale systems that prove an approach and give you something real to evaluate.

Classification and Prediction

Models that sort records, flag likely outcomes or forecast a number from your historical data.

Computer Vision

Image classification, object detection and visual inspection prototypes using transfer learning.

Text and Language

Text classification, search, summarisation and assistant-style interfaces over your own documents.

Data Preparation

Cleaning, labelling and structuring the data a model needs — usually the largest part of the work.

Evaluation and Dashboards

Metrics you can interpret, plus a dashboard to compare models and show results clearly.

Model Handover

Reproducible scripts, trained model files and documentation of limits, assumptions and next steps.

HydroPonics, listed on the Demos page, is an example of a built project that combines sensor readings with automated control.

Honest Scope

Prototype First, Production Later

Most AI work starts as a prototype: a model that proves an approach on your data. That is genuinely useful — it tells you whether the idea is worth investing in before you spend on infrastructure.

A production system needs more: more data, monitoring, retraining, failure handling and hosting. If your project needs that, the quote will say so plainly rather than pretending a prototype is the finished thing.

Measured, Not Promised

We report the baseline, the final metrics and the cases where the model fails. A prototype that is honest about its limits is more useful than a demo that only shows the wins.

How We Work

Five Steps, With Evidence at Each One

You see the data, the baseline and the metrics — not just a final demo.

Frame the problem

Define the decision the model supports and what a useful result would look like in practice.

Prepare the data

Clean, label and split the dataset, and document where it came from and what it cannot represent.

Build a baseline

Start with the simplest model that could work, so improvements are measured against something real.

Evaluate honestly

Report accuracy, error and failure cases — including the results that are inconvenient.

Hand over

Reproducible code, a working interface or notebook, and a walkthrough of limits and next steps.

What Your Project Needs

Model quality follows data quality. These are the questions we ask before promising a result.

  • Enough examples for the question being asked, not just a handful
  • Permission to use the data, and a plan for anything sensitive
  • Labels that are consistent, or a labelling plan we can follow
  • An understanding of what “correct” means for your use case
  • A realistic view of what a prototype can and cannot promise

Have a Problem Worth Testing?

Describe the decision you are trying to support and the data you have. We will tell you honestly whether ML is the right tool.
